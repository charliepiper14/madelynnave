The Rule That Replaces It

Nothing gets a return decision on data less than seven days old. Pruning still happens fast, but on cost per session, cart rate and session to checkout, which mature in two or three days. Where a call has to come sooner it is made on spend against a cap, which is the mechanism Oscar proposed and it is the right one. In platform return stays a floor by design, because any cycle past seven days never reaches Meta at all.

The Arithmetic, And It Decides The Next Three Months

Test budget is $435 a day, which is $3,045 a week. At $700 per readable ad that is 4.3 ads a week that can be taken to a purchase decision. Sixteen ads need $11,200 to all clear the gate, which at the current test budget is twenty six days. So a sixteen ad batch is not a test, it is a queue, and most of the queue never gets read at all. The two that sold this week did so on $38.49 and $123.39, which is luck of the draw rather than evidence, and the three biggest spenders in the batch produced nothing.

What I Am Proposing, And What It Buys Us

Four to five new ads a week, each landing in a cell that is already selling, with more angles inside fewer ads rather than more ads carrying one angle each. On the same $435 a day every new ad reaches $700 inside eight days, so we get a real verdict weekly instead of a backlog. Creative volume still matters to Meta, and we hold that with copy rotation, which we already run at three variants per ad, and with the Zombie lane cycling proven assets every twenty one days. Throughput without readability is the thing to avoid, not throughput.

Why This Happens, And What We Do About It

Maximise Conversions on a cell with no purchase history buys the cheapest available click, because it has no signal telling it what a buyer looks like. That produces impressive top line numbers, a $15.29 CPM, a 3.30 percent click rate and 2,291 sessions for $1,236.86, and almost none of it is a person buying a thousand dollar hand painted piece. The lanes with purchase history pay two to four times more per session and convert five to twelve times better. From here a low CPM in a test lane is a flag to investigate, not a result to celebrate, and I will report cost per session alongside CPM every week so the two are never confused again.

The Two Landing Pages, Separated As Promised

The Christian Collection page cell returned 7.42x on $176.61 and converted 2.88 percent of its sessions to checkout, above the 1.50 percent account rate and fifteen times the 0.19 percent the old advertorial pages managed last week. The Architectural Digest page cell spent $180.50 on the same $35 daily budget, bought 133 sessions and produced no checkouts at all. Same audience, same week, same budget, two different pages. The Collection page is a keeper. The AD page has $180.50 of its $700 allowance spent and runs on under the gate, but I would not bet on it.

The Compare Cell, An Honest Reversal

Last week I reported Compare Faith Taste Proof as the best of the July refresh at 1.98x on day five. I expect it to fail that and I would rather say so now than present it again as a survivor.
