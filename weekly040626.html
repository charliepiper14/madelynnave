This Is The Post Promo Reversion We Flagged Last Week, Not A Structural Break

Last week's deck called the read directly, the job had shifted from recovering to holding an elevated base, and that promo level base was never going to hold once the two for one offer ended on May 31. This window covers four fading promo days and three days with no offer at all, so the drop to 1.20x is the expected shape of a sale ending rather than the account breaking. Two things make the number look worse than the underlying truth. Spend stayed near full at $1,220 a day while the offer disappeared, which left roughly $3,900 running on promo dependent creative that no longer had an offer behind it. And the pull was taken today, June 4, so the final three days sit inside the attribution window and will revise up as late purchases attribute.

Strip The Two Week Promo Distortion And The Trend Is Exactly Where It Was Tracking

Before the promo the account had climbed four straight weeks, 1.22x to 1.60x to 2.14x to 2.60x. The BOGO spiked it to 6.38x for one week, then it gave that spike back. Underneath the noise the durable carriers held in the mid 2x range, Abide With Me returned 4.62x and the Fine Art Combined Grid 2.60x, both non promo evergreen units. That is the real base, and it sits right where the pre promo trend pointed, comfortably profitable against the $424 breakeven and far above April. The month closing at 3.19x on lower spend than April is the headline that matters, and it is covered in full on slides 6 and 7.

Read The Steps, This Is An Offer Story Not A Site Story

The cart to checkout step held almost flat at 35.6% against 37.2%, which tells us the on site experience between cart and checkout did not break. The break is at checkout to purchase, 32.0% down to 13.8%, and at click to cart, 12.3% down to 7.1%. Both are the steps an offer touches most directly. People still browse, still add, still reach checkout, then more of them stall at the final commit once the two for one reason to buy now has gone. That is the textbook shape of a promo ending, and it is why the same funnel that looked fixed last week looks soft this week without anything structural having changed.

Why The Eden Landing Page Matters More Now

The checkout to purchase weakness is exactly the gap a dedicated landing page is built to close, removing navigation, handling the objections the brief named around commitment and whether the piece suits the room, and carrying intent through to the commit without leaning on a discount. With the promo gone the account is back on the clean non promo baseline that makes an A and B test trustworthy, so the week of June 2 is the right window. Eden is the right subject, it is the proven anchor by order volume and the one whose cold performance most needs a conversion lift that is not offer dependent.

The Real Story Of May Is Efficiency, Not The Promo Week

It would be easy to read May as one big promo week carrying the month, but the numbers say otherwise. Spend came down 15% from April while revenue rose 58% and purchases rose from 77 to 127. That is the account doing more with less, the definition of the scaling the brief asked for. CPA almost halved from $567 to $292, moving from above the $424 breakeven in April to comfortably below it in May. Every one of the four campaigns improved its ROAS month on month, so the gain is broad based rather than one campaign masking the rest. May is the first month under management where the account cleared profitability across the board.

3.19x Sits Just Under The 3.5x Target, And The Path To Close The Gap Is Clear

The 3.5x ROAS target was the standard set at onboarding, and May landed at 3.19x, within touching distance on the first full month of the recovery. The gap is held open by two campaigns, the legacy Fine Art ABO at 2.31x and Evergreen at 1.66x, both of which the June plan addresses directly through consolidation and the ABO rebuild. TLIMS at 3.99x and Fine Art Value Opt at 3.58x are already past target. Move budget toward those two structures and retire the drag, and 3.5x for the month becomes a realistic June or July milestone on the durable base alone, before any further promo lift.

Action Plan And Path To Scale

Cut The Leakers, Right Size The Spend, Concentrate On The Proven Carriers Into June

Immediate, This Week

  • Pause LastSupper Refresh Video in Evergreen now. $1,166 spent at 0.02x with 52 checkouts and almost no revenue, the single worst unit on the account this week, ad level pause carries no learning reset so this is a clean cut today
  • Pause the Fine Art Carousel in the legacy campaign. 0.19x on $1,186 and well past its 7 day window, it is dragging the campaign that consolidation is already set to retire
  • Step Eden Static back, do not kill it. The product still sells, but its cold spend at 0.18x without an offer should drop to a holding level until the landing page test gives it a non promo conversion path
  • Confirm the Memorial Day ad set is fully off. The offer expired May 31, any residual delivery on the BOGO trio is spending against a sale that no longer exists

Right Size The Daily Budget

  • Apply the 25% rule downward at ad set level. Spend held near $1,220 a day into a no offer week, step it down toward the non promo base so the account is not buying at promo volume without the promo return
  • Reallocate to the three proven carriers. Abide With Me at 4.62x, the Combined Grid at 2.60x and the Reflections refresh at 2.37x take the freed budget, these are the units working on product and context alone
  • Hold campaign budgets steady for 48 hours first. The last three days are still attributing, let the week firm up before any budget level change that would reset learning
