The Ordering Is Now Perfect, And That Matters

Rank the five campaigns by cost per session and you get the same order as ranking them by return, by session to cart and by session to checkout. Eden Scale paid $4.57 a session, converted 5.00 percent of them to checkout and returned 4.17x. The Christian sandbox paid $1.79, converted 0.46 percent and returned 0.19x. There is no campaign that breaks the pattern in either direction. A conversion campaign with no purchase history buys the cheapest available click because it has nothing telling it what a buyer looks like, and on a $1,000 hand painted piece the cheapest click is almost never the buyer.

What We Do With It

Low CPM and cheap sessions in a new cell are a warning, not a result. From here I report cost per session next to CPM every week so the two are never confused, and a new cell buying traffic below about $2.00 a session gets looked at within 3 days instead of running on to a spend gate. It is a faster and cheaper signal than waiting on a purchase verdict, maturing in 2 to 3 days against 14. On this week's data it would have flagged the Christian sandbox in the first 72 hours instead of after $3,082.

The One Reversal I Should Name

Faith Taste Proof was reported last week as a $600 gate kill on zero purchases. Read against the full 30 days instead of a 14 day window it does have one order, at $804.80, so it never met the zero purchase rule. It was killed instead under the $1,400 threshold, at $1,336.38 and 0.60x, which is the correct rule for it. Same outcome, different justification, and the lesson is that the gate has to read lifetime spend and lifetime purchases or it produces the wrong answer on ads that sold early and then stalled.
