What Actually Happened

Four things, in order of size. The BOGO left the site at 23:59 on 7 September, so this is the first week in three without an offer behind it, and store discounts fell from $67,770 to $9,665. Session to cart went 6.48 to 3.52 percent on the store's own numbers, which is the fall I forecast in the last two decks. Christian Scale CBO collapsed from 6.49x to 1.05x. The Christian recycle cell went from 11.72x to 1.53x. Set against that, Fine Art main held at 8.04x and actually improved, and the store still took 47 orders and $36,199 of net sales.

The Store Fell Less Than The Ad Account

Meta orders halved from 71 to 35. Shopify orders fell from 93 to 47, a 49.5 percent fall, so the two moved together. But net sales fell from $101,099 to $36,199 while Meta attributed revenue fell from $81,222 to $32,683. Meta now accounts for 90.3 percent of store net revenue against 80.3 percent last week, which means the ad account is carrying more of the business, not less. The problem is volume, not attribution.

Basket Fell Because The Discount Did

Meta basket fell 18.4 percent to $933.79. Store net AOV fell from $1,087 to $770. The gap between those two numbers is the discount, and it moved from 40.1 percent of gross sales to 21.1 percent. Meta counts order value before the code is applied, the store counts it after. With the store figures alongside, the contribution model on slide 8 now runs on what the business actually banks.

All previous contribution figures in earlier decks should be read as approximately 20 percent high.

Risk And Data Integrity

4 Things That Could Make This Week Read Worse or Better Than It Was, and One Correction to Something I Told You on Tuesday

None of these are errors. They are the reasons a 3.40x week should not be treated as the new level any more than 7.15x should have been.

1. The Window Is Wide Open On This One

Every day of this period still sits inside the 7 day click window, where last week's figure had a settled first half. Last week's 7.15x settled at 7.14x, so the drift on that period was small, but a week where every day is still open is a floor in a way that a week with three settled days is not. Expect this number to rise, and expect the rise to be larger than last week's did.

2. Revenue Concentration Got Worse

2 ads produced 56.0 percent of the week's revenue against a top 3 of 43.4 percent last week. Grid alone took 32.6 percent from 7 orders. At 35 orders a week two large baskets move the account several points in either direction. The same concentration that made last week look exceptional is making this week look worse than the underlying position.

3. Nine New Ads Were In Learning

The 16 September batch took $475.75 and the revival wave took $543.40, so $1,019.15 or 10.6 percent of the week sat in ads with one day of delivery and no optimisation history. Link click through fell from 2.12 to 1.90 percent, which is consistent with that. A tenth of the budget was spent on ads the algorithm did not yet know how to place.

4. Attribution Is Still Split

Two live cells run the wider window including 1 day engaged view and the rest run 7 day click or 1 day view. That remains unconsolidated and it remains on the list. On a week this small the effect is larger in proportional terms than usual, so some of the lane difference on slide 12 is measurement rather than performance.

Correction: Juan's Partnership Ad Did Take An Order

On Tuesday I told you Juan was at $522 with no purchases and would hit the $600 gate. He was paused on 16 September at $600.31, and by then the ad had taken 1 order at a $462.18 basket. So the raw data shows a purchase where my message said there were none, and you will see that if you look. The pause was still correct under our own rule, because $462.18 sits below the $672 account basket floor and an order below the floor counts as no purchase for gate purposes. But the message should have said that rather than stating there were no purchases at all, and I would rather flag it here than have you find it.
