Why This Week Worked

Three things happened at once. The 4 ads carrying dead weight came off on 1 September, so a quarter of the budget stopped buying nothing. What The Margin Position Allows

Every order bought this week carries $618.73 of contribution after the media that bought it. Cost per order would have to rise from $247.26 to $865.99, a 250 percent increase, before an order stopped paying for itself. Even on August's full month basket of $983.12 the breakeven sits at $792.97 and we bought at $404.81. There is no margin case for restraint at $1,500 a day, and there has not been one for some time.

Where This Sits Against The Targets

The original targets were 3.5x and a $150 to $310 cost per acquisition, set against a $560 basket where breakeven was $424. This week delivered 4.33x at $247.26, so both targets were met at once for the first time. On today's basket, 3.5x implies a $305.81 cost per order, which is inside the original CPA range. The two targets have converged instead of pulling against each other.

The Number To Keep Perspective On

This is one week, on a floor reading, with 2 promo days inside it. August as a whole delivered $41,145 of contribution across 31 days, or $1,327 a day, and this week ran at $3,536 a day. That gap is too large to be a new baseline. What it does establish is a ceiling worth aiming at, and the fact that the account clears its economics with room to spare even in a poor week.

Risk And Data Integrity

4 Things That Could Make This Week Read Better Than It Was, Stated Before Anyone Builds a Plan on It

The BOGO went live on 1 September and sits on the site, so the last 2 of 7 days carry an offer that lifted cart rate across every lane. Session to cart went 6.98 to 16.79 percent account wide.
